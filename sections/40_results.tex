\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We evaluate on the scenes of Table~\ref{tab:scenes}: DCR's own road, dinner, and
ledge figure layouts --- re-simulated with the two-way modal-constraint coupling in
place of DCR's one-way injection --- plus two modal-network stress scenes
(a box--box cube stack and a two-body full-FEM reference). Reusing DCR's layouts is
deliberate: it lets us show, on the same scenes, the back-reaction DCR cannot
produce (a distant body moved \emph{by} another body's ring through their shared
contact).

Table~\ref{tab:matrix} is the validation matrix. Each row is a metric scored against
a named reference baseline; each column is a scene. \yes\ marks a measured result
(number in the cell, subsection cited); \pend\ marks a measurement planned against
the full-FEM ground truth currently being wired; \na\ marks not-applicable. The
matrix doubles as the experiment tracker --- the honest current state is that the
contact-force ledger, the two-way network discriminator, the passivity
bound/ablation (three scenes), the restitution sweep, the slab full-FEM anchor,
and runtime on three scenes are measured; the cross-scene FEM-accuracy rows, the
momentum probe, and everything on the road scene are pending.

\paragraph*{Baselines (the reference axis).} Metrics are scored against:
\textbf{F} --- a full-FEM (unreduced) solve of the same mesh, the accuracy ground
truth \note{(consider IPC~\citep{lan2022abd}-grade contact for the reference; a
penalty/linear-implicit FEM under-resolves stiff contact)};
\textbf{D} --- DCR one-way (rigid $+$ separate IIR, no back-reaction), the base
method;
\textbf{R} --- rigid-only (modes off), the cost floor and the two-way null;
\textbf{A} --- the analytic static ledger ($\sum\lambda = mg$);
and the two internal toggles \textbf{off/on} (passivity clamp; modal network) that
isolate the two claims that are ours alone.

\subsection{Two-way through the network}
\label{sec:results:network}
The \texttt{upper} cube touches only \texttt{mid}; with the modal contact network on
it rings ($|\amp|$ peak $\approx 1.1\times10^{-5}$), off it is identically zero. The
vibration reaching it arrives only through the shared contacts. The modal compliance
also \emph{smooths} the box--box force (joint-force ripple $\pm2.72$~N off
$\to \pm1.48$~N on, mean unchanged at $mg$).

\subsection{Full-FEM ground truth}
\label{sec:results:fem}
On the slab reference (one corner-fixed $10\times6\times2$ FEM slab carrying an
impactor and a bystander; both arms integrate the \emph{same} operator, the
native arm using its true eigenmodes), the reduced-vs-full-FEM mid-deflection
ratio converges $0.56 \to 0.75 \to 0.90 \to 1.03$ as the rigid step refines
$h = 1/120 \to 1/960$: the coupling magnitude is \emph{correct}, and the gap at
the paper step is the temporal resolution of a sub-millisecond impulse
delivered over one $8.3$~ms step, not a coupling error. The ring frequency
matches at $81.0$ (GT) vs $80.7$~Hz (native), $0.4\%$ --- read at $h=1/480$,
since at the paper step the $81$~Hz ring aliases against the $120$~Hz sampling
(the modes carry $\omega_j$ exactly at every $h$ via $K_q$). Truncation is not
the bottleneck: the distant deflection field is captured by the first
${\sim}2$ bending modes (field error $35.8\%$ at $k{=}1$, ${\approx}27$--$30\%$
for $k\ge2$ --- the plateau is the timestep floor above, not mode count). Two
honesty notes calibrate everything else we report. First, the trustworthy GT
observable is the \emph{surface deflection field}: the bystander's launch KE
does \emph{not} converge under GT refinement ($158\to39\to69$~mJ as
$h_{\mathrm{GT}}$ halves --- a ${\sim}2$~kHz penalty-contact oscillator the GT
resolves poorly) while the field is stable to $1\%$; launch quantities are
therefore secondary observables for both arms. Second, the validated falloff
is the slab's low-mode \emph{standing-wave} profile --- non-monotonic with
distance and reproduced by the native arm with no $C\,r^{-\beta}$ term at
every $h$ --- not traveling-wave decay; the no-attenuation-term advantage is
claimed for this modal regime only. At the paper step the native arm costs
$13.6\times$ less wall-clock than the full-FEM GT on this scene.
A tet-refinement ladder on the ground-truth meshes (R0--R4, up to
67k nodes per support) shows the linear-tet operators themselves are still
$3$--$10\%$/rung from the continuum (CST bending lock releases slowly), so all
ground-truth frequencies and sags are reported as properties of the
\emph{shared discrete operator}, never as physical plate values --- both arms
use the same operator, so discretization bias cancels from every ratio.

On the dinner scene (DCR \S5.1 duplicate: their Table~2 material, 5~kg pot,
dishes rigid exactly as in their \S4.5), the pot is dropped at three points
along the 2.2~m table against an all-FEM ground truth in which \emph{every}
body is elastic (implicit Newmark at $h=5\times10^{-5}$~s, ${\sim}18$~min per
1.2~s drop vs.\ ${\sim}34$~ms/step for ours, \S\ref{sec:results:perf}). The two arms agree on the operator ---
the ground truth's measured ring ($9.2$--$9.9$~Hz) matches the native basis
$f_1 = 9.8$~Hz --- and the native response falls off with distance from the
drop point with \emph{no fitted attenuation model} (DCR fits its Eq.~14
$C,\beta$ by hand or to data; Fig.~\ref{fig:dinner}). The native arm
under-predicts absolute dish-launch amplitude by ${\sim}7\times$: measured to
be insensitive to mode count ($24\to48$) and under-relaxation
($0.7\to1.0$), the deficit is structural --- implicit solver-rate modal
integration band-limits the impact transient whose surface velocity launches
dishes (ballistic height $\propto v^2$). DCR's IIR-plus-kick architecture
(their Eqs.~10--12) sub-steps the filter and re-injects the \emph{maximum}
displacement as a velocity change, deliberately re-amplifying that transient
--- and thereby exceeding the passive bound as $\varepsilon_r\!\to\!1$ (X7);
our formulation makes the opposite trade.

\subsection{Head-to-head with DCR on one operator}
\label{sec:results:dcr}
Figure~\ref{fig:x2falloff} runs the slab reference under three arms --- rigid-only
(\textbf{R}), paper-DCR (\textbf{D}: the Eq.~10 forced IIR plus the post-solve
$\Delta v = d_{\max}/h$ kick), and the native constraint --- and measures each
bystander's peak KE against its distance from an off-centre impact. Rigid-only
produces no distant response (identically zero beyond the nearest object: the
machinery is genuinely off). Paper-DCR is strongest near the impact
($13.5$~mJ at $0.14$~m) but collapses to $0.9$~mJ by $0.42$~m. The native arm
starts softer ($9.8$~mJ) and falls off gently, delivering $2.0$~mJ at
$0.56$~m --- $2.3\times$ the paper-DCR response at the farthest object; the two
curves \emph{cross}. The native falloff is the slab's standing-wave modal
profile (\S\ref{sec:results:fem}) with no $C\,r^{-\beta}$ term and no per-scene
response parameters (paper-DCR hand-sets $C,\beta,r_0$ and its Rayleigh pair);
its steeper decay is the impulse-localized forced response. Honesty: this is a
method-level comparison on one shared operator, each arm at its natural
timestep --- the comparand is the falloff \emph{shape}, which is
$h$-independent (\S\ref{sec:results:fem}), not absolute magnitude.

\subsection{The passivity bound bites when it must}
\label{sec:results:passivity}
Two experiments separate ``naturally passive'' from ``needs the governor'':
an adversarial budget$\times$relaxation sweep, and the budgets solvers
actually ship.

\paragraph*{Adversarial sweep (Fig.~\ref{fig:x1robust}).} XPBD (the injecting
formulation, \S\ref{sec:method:solve}) on three scenes (shelf, ledge, dinner)
$\times$ relax $\{0.7, 1.0\}$ $\times$ budget $\{4{\times}1, 8{\times}2,
16{\times}4, 32{\times}8\}$, clamp OFF vs ON; ``passivity'' is the peak
support-ring energy over the impactor KE. OFF: \textbf{12 of 24 cells inject},
with per-scene worst cases $1.1\times10^{4}$ (shelf), $1.2\times10^{5}$
(ledge), $5.1\times10^{3}$ (dinner). ON: \textbf{all 24 cells satisfy the
ledger invariant}, and every injecting cell lands at $\le 1.22$. At the paper
configuration ($16{\times}4$, relax $0.7$) the clamp fires \textbf{zero times
on all three scenes} and the trajectories are unchanged --- the governor is
inert exactly where the solver is already passive. On the shelf drop the
enforcement is machine-tight: the un-clamped $8{\times}2$ ring peaks at
$53.7\times$ the impactor KE, the clamped run sits at $1.01$, and the maximum
net ledger excess is ${\sim}3\times10^{-17}$~J.

\paragraph*{Production budgets.} At the substep-heavy budgets XPBD ships
($1{\times}16$, $2{\times}4$, $1{\times}8$) the un-governed modal block
injects $5.5\times10^{4}$ to $9.2\times10^{6}$~J into a scene holding
${\sim}1$~J; the clamp bounds every case to $36$--$42$~J. AVBD at
$4{\times}4$ / $2{\times}4$ is \emph{bit-identical} with the clamp off or on
($0.91$/$0.79$~J, zero activations): on the descent-class solver passivity is
free and the ledger is a witness, not an actor --- the dichotomy of
\S\ref{sec:method:solve} measured.

\paragraph*{Enforcement as shipped.} The budget each substep is the
\emph{irreversible} contact loss, $\mathrm{grav\ work} - \Delta \mathrm{KE}$
(zero in ballistic flight, positive only when contact dissipates); losses
accumulate in a reservoir so the guarantee is cumulative,
$\sum \dEmod \le \eta \sum \dErig$, and stays inert while slack remains; a
substep that would overshoot is projected back by scaling the modal state
$(\amp,\dot\amp)$ by a factor $\gamma$ with
$\gamma^2 = (E_{\mathrm{old}}+\mathrm{budget})/E_{\mathrm{new}}$, landing
exactly on the ceiling. \note{Method pass: state
in \S\ref{sec:method:passivity} that the shipped clamp is this cumulative,
full-state generalization of the per-step velocity-kick bound of
Eq.~\ref{eq:alphastar} --- same budget, projection of $(\amp,\dot\amp)$ rather
than a scale on the kick alone.}

\paragraph*{The price, honestly.} The ledger costs $+0.5$--$4.8$~ms/step at
the paper configuration (single-run; re-measured with repetitions in the
performance pass) and up to $+37$--$64\%$ on XPBD at extreme substep counts
--- host read-backs in the energy accounting, an implementation cost, not
fundamental. The physical price is one-sided: on AVBD the clamp never fires
(zero cost); on XPBD it prevents the blow-up but \emph{over-damps} the ring
--- cap-ON $|\amp|\approx1.2\times10^{-5}$ vs $6\times10^{-4}$ for converged
XPBD and $8\times10^{-5}$ for the FEM-validated AVBD reference --- because
the injected state is $\dot\amp$-heavy and the $\gamma$ projection shrinks
$\amp$ with it. The cap makes the livelier host \emph{stable}, not
\emph{accurate}; AVBD is the accurate default on which the guarantee is free.

\subsection{Restitution: bounded where the forced IIR double-counts}
\label{sec:results:restitution}
DCR's own \S5.4 flags the flaw: as $\varepsilon_r\to1$ a contact dissipates
almost nothing, so a response driven by the contact \emph{impulse} keeps
injecting vibration energy that no longer exists to give. We sweep the pot's
$\varepsilon_r$ on the dinner drop and measure, per impact, injected vibration
energy over rigid contact loss (Fig.~\ref{fig:x7}). Paper-DCR: the loss
collapses $7.30\to0.20$~J while the forced-IIR injection \emph{grows}
$0.15\to0.52$~J; the ratio crosses $1$ near $\varepsilon_r\approx0.8$ and
plateaus at $2.63$--$2.64\times$ --- energy created. Native: $0.053$ at every
$\varepsilon_r$ --- the clamp funds modal gain from measured rigid loss, so
restitution cannot break the bound \emph{by construction}. Two honesty notes:
the accounting is windowed per impact, so the exact crossing point is
scene/window-dependent (the trend --- loss vanishes, injection persists --- is
the robust signal); and the native contact is $e{=}0$ (no restitution term in
the native solver yet), so its curve is the bounded reference, not a
matched-restitution comparison.

\subsection{Runtime at the paper configuration}
\label{sec:results:perf}
Table~\ref{tab:perf} gives CPU-host timings at the paper configuration
(single machine, 100 frames after warm-up, symplectic modal path --- the
configuration every result above validates). AVBD is $1.6$--$3.1\times$
faster than XPBD here (XPBD pays a per-mode Gauss--Seidel sweep;
dinner at 17 bodies / 24 modes is its worst case). The honest split behind
the title: the fastest validated configuration (ledge/AVBD, $15.8$~ms,
${\sim}63$~steps/s) is \emph{interactive} but not $120$~Hz real-time; a
device-resident, non-symplectic path of the same solver measures
$0.9$--$2.4$~ms/step but is a different code path that the passivity
validation does not yet cover, so we claim interactive rates only. For
context, the full-FEM ground truth costs $13.6\times$ the native arm on the
slab scene (\S\ref{sec:results:fem}).
