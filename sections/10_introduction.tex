\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph*{Motivation.} Rigid-body simulators are fast because they discard
deformation, but real objects ring, chatter, and transmit vibration through their
contacts. Interactive applications obtain contact-rich scenes from position-based
and augmented-Lagrangian solvers run at fixed, low iteration budgets
(XPBD~\citep{macklin2016xpbd}, VBD/AVBD~\citep{chen2024vbd,giles2025avbd}); a
full soft-body discretization in such a host costs thousands of degrees of
freedom per body, while modal reduction restores vibration response for six
rigid plus a handful of modal coordinates. Cheap reduced coordinates are not
the hard part --- subspace simulation is decades old (\S\ref{sec:related}). The
open questions are how tightly the modal layer is \emph{coupled} to contact ---
whether contact merely \emph{reads} rigid motion and adds a cosmetic vibration
(one-way), or whether the vibration \emph{reacts} back on the bodies and on
their neighbours through the same contact (two-way) --- and whether that
coupling can be made \emph{safe} in a solver that is never run to convergence.

\paragraph*{The gap.} \citet{zheng2011} resolve modal vibration inside both the
collision and frictional-contact stages, producing genuinely coupled motions --- but
at large computational cost, in an offline velocity-level solver.
\citet{coevoet2020distant} make this affordable for real time by dropping the
back-reaction: they inject a distant collision response as a one-way velocity change
and the simulation ``remains rigid.'' Reduced bodies with two-way contact do run
at interactive rates --- Affine Body Dynamics~\citep{lan2022abd} --- but on an
affine basis that cannot bend or ring, inside a Newton/barrier solver rather
than the fixed-budget family. And none of the three offers a guarantee on the
\emph{energy} the contact injects into the reduced coordinates.

\paragraph*{This paper.} We embed the vibration modes as native compliant constraints
inside an interactive-rate augmented-Lagrangian / position-based rigid solver
(XPBD, VBD/AVBD). One
unilateral contact multiplier excites the modes and reacts on the bodies within the
same solver iteration (\S\ref{sec:method}). We are explicit about what that step is
\emph{not}: the constraint is no new contact law --- linearized, the position-level
row recovers the velocity-level coupling of \citet{zheng2011}
(Eq.~\ref{eq:velequiv}) --- so the transcription itself is one line, and the
contribution begins where it fails. The fixed-budget regime \emph{creates} a failure
mode the convergent offline solver never had: an under-converged local solve injects
spurious energy into the stiff modal block, or diverges outright. Our remedy is
twofold: solver couplings that keep the stiff block stable at a fixed budget --- an
engaged-gated implicit modal block under relaxation in the augmented-Lagrangian host,
per-mode compliant rows under a tighter measured relaxation ceiling in the
position-based one (\S\ref{sec:method:solve}) --- and an enforced passivity bound
$\dEmod \le \eta\,\dErig$ on the contact$\to$modal transfer, maintained cumulatively
every substep by an exact energy projection (\S\ref{sec:method:passivity}), which
holds however far the host converged. We generalize the invariant across a
body--body modal contact network and validate against a full-FEM ground truth
(\S\ref{sec:results}). The result runs where its audience does: a GPU-resident
co-solved path reaches $120$~Hz real-time rates and sustains $512$ bodies
while a per-run ledger verifies the same bound, and --- as a demonstration,
not a contribution --- the bounded excitation stream drives a standard modal
impact-sound render (\S\ref{sec:results:perf}).

\paragraph*{Contributions.} These are one thesis, not independent claims: the
fixed-budget regime \emph{creates} the injection failure mode a convergent
global solve never faced, and the enforced bound is what closes it --- the
constraint row itself is claimed by neither.
\begin{itemize}
  \item A cumulative \textbf{directional energy budget} on the transfer from
        contact into the modes, funded by measured rigid contact loss and
        enforced by an exact modal-state projection
        (\S\ref{sec:method:passivity}). Prior energy-stable contact constrains
        the \emph{system} --- non-increase, whole-system passivity, or
        conservation projection (\S\ref{sec:related}) --- not the one-sided,
        source-referenced transfer.
  \item \textbf{Stable fixed-budget solver couplings} for the stiff modal
        block. The constraint row itself is a position-level transcription
        of a classical law (Eq.~\ref{eq:velequiv}); transcribed naively it
        injects energy or diverges at production iteration budgets. We
        develop the treatments that survive truncation --- an engaged-gated
        implicit modal block with under-relaxation in the
        augmented-Lagrangian host, per-mode compliant rows with a measured
        relaxation ceiling in the position-based one --- and show the
        textbook alternative, cross-term Schur condensation, is
        net-injecting at the same budget (\S\ref{sec:method:solve}).
  \item That bound \textbf{generalized across a body--body modal contact
        network} --- stacked bodies ring through their shared contacts.
  \item Validation against a full-FEM ground truth and a contact-force audit,
        plus interactive-rate capability: a GPU-resident co-solved path at
        $120$~Hz real-time budgets with sublinear ${\approx}N^{1/2}$ scaling
        to $512$ bodies, the passivity ledger verified in every measured cell
        (\S\ref{sec:results}).
\end{itemize}
