\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We embedded vibration modes as first-class compliant-constraint DOFs in a real-time
augmented-Lagrangian rigid solver, driven two-way by a shared unilateral contact
multiplier, and made the coupling stable at a fixed low iteration budget with an
enforced passivity bound $\dEmod\le\eta\,\dErig$.

\paragraph*{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The two-way coupling \emph{mechanism} is not new
        (\citealp{zheng2011,hauser2003,barbic2008haptic,sheth2015}), and
        neither are energy projection or quadratic energy clamps
        (\citealp{su2009energy,dinev2018fepr}); the contribution is the shared
        fixed-budget AL embedding and the directional, source-referenced
        energy bound.
  \item The bound applies to the modal-path injection only; the
        spatial-attenuation path is empirical and carries no energy budget.
  \item Small-displacement regime only: support contact rows sample the modal
        basis at each contact's rest footprint with a fixed vertical normal
        (DCR instead re-maps contacts barycentrically per step), and the
        linearized modes carry no geometric stiffening --- valid at the
        mm-scale responses shown, not for span-scale deflections.
  \item The rigid step band-limits the impact transient: dish-launch
        amplitudes run well under the full-FEM reference at $h=1/120$ and
        recover monotonically with the step ($16\times$ over an $8\times$
        ladder, \S\ref{sec:results:fem}) without reaching it --- though the
        reference launch observable is itself contact-model-limited, so the
        residual is an upper bound on the true deficit. DCR's sub-stepped
        forced-IIR response re-amplifies that transient at the cost of passivity
        (\S\ref{sec:results:restitution}).
  \item Two paths, one invariant: the passivity- and accuracy-validated
        correctness path is the symplectic modal step (CPU; $11$--$34$~ms/step
        on a laptop-class core, $3.5$--$4.6\times$ slower on the server core
        of Table~\ref{tab:perf} --- single-core-bound), while the real-time
        path is the same solver's
        device-resident co-solved step ($5.6$--$9.2$~ms at the paper budget,
        real-time at a lighter budget; \S\ref{sec:results:perf}). A read-only
        ledger \emph{measures} the co-solved path passive across all four
        scenes and five budgets with no active clamp --- verified per run,
        not enforced (the $\gamma$-projection is not yet device-resident).
        The open device items are wiring the $O(k)$ enforcement device-side
        and reproducing the deflection-field accuracy validation on the
        co-solved step --- the reason we claim interactive rather than
        real-time rates.
  \item Native contact restitution is $e{=}0$: the sweep of
        \S\ref{sec:results:restitution} shows the energy ratio stays bounded at
        every $\varepsilon_r$ by construction, but a matched-restitution
        comparison against DCR needs a Newton restitution term in the native
        contact --- future work.
  \item Scene breadth: the road scene is qualitative-only (timed on the
        device path; excluded from the matrix); the $N$-body stress grid
        (\S\ref{sec:results:perf}) measures runtime and passivity to $513$
        bodies but carries no accuracy rows (it has no FEM twin); and the
        affine (ABD) basis shows a slow secular buildup and would need the
        same governor before any stability claim.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph*{Future work.} A swappable affine (ABD) cargo basis for cheap, robust
contact, energy-governed the same way; a Newton restitution term in the native
contact so the restitution comparison against DCR
(\S\ref{sec:results:restitution}) can be run at matched $\varepsilon_r$ rather
than against the $e{=}0$ reference; a ground-truth accuracy pass on the road
scene; and the two device items of \S\ref{sec:results:perf}, which stand
between the measured interactive rates and a real-time claim.
