\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph*{Modal contact.} \citet{zheng2011} carry modal amplitudes as
generalized coordinates driven two-way by a shared contact multiplier, resolving
vibration in both the collision and friction stages; the coupling is
momentum-consistent by construction but solved offline as a global staggered LCP/QP.
Crucially, their large cost buys \emph{audio}: they target high-quality contact
\emph{sound}, resolving vibration at a far finer temporal grid than a visual-rate
simulation needs. We therefore do not claim to make their method real-time --- we
target a different, coarser regime (visual-rate two-way coupling, no synthesis), so
the comparison is one of regime, not a speedup of the same computation.
The relation is in fact closer than a shared goal: linearized, our
position-level normal row \emph{is} their velocity-level contact condition up
to a stabilization bias (Eq.~\ref{eq:velequiv}) --- and theirs is the broader
law, coupling modes through friction where ours uses the normal rows only. We
therefore claim no novelty in the constraint; the claims live in what a
fixed-iteration-budget host does to that row, and in the energy bound.
Interactive two-way modal contact itself predates real-time AL solvers:
\citet{hauser2003} impose manipulation and collision constraints directly on a
rigid-frame-plus-modes model at interactive rates, and \citet{barbic2008haptic}
render distributed penalty contact between reduced (modal-capable) deformable
models at haptic kilohertz rates. Neither shares a unilateral
augmented-Lagrangian multiplier between rigid and modal DOFs at a fixed
iteration budget, and neither bounds the contact$\to$modal energy transfer.
\citet{james2002dyrt} render one-way real-time modal response (display-only, no
back-reaction).
Closest in spirit, \citet{coevoet2020distant} approximate the response of
distant contacts one-way in real time: precomputed modes drive a forced IIR
whose peak displacement is re-injected as a velocity change, and the
simulation remains rigid. That work is the immediate motivation for ours,
which replaces the injected response with vibration state the contact solve
carries natively.

\paragraph*{Reduced bodies as contact DOFs.} Affine Body
Dynamics~\citep{lan2022abd} makes a $9$-DOF affine subspace a native contact DOF
inside IPC; it captures uniform stretch/shear but not the bending/plate modes that
carry the far-field response, and carries no energy-transfer bound.
\citet{sheth2015} give fully momentum-conserving reduced deformable contact ---
two-way and conserving \emph{momentum}, but not bounding \emph{energy}. Classical
floating-frame / component-mode multibody (\citealp{shabana_multibody};
energy--momentum integrators, \citealp{betsch2007}) couple modal reduction with
contact but \emph{conserve} energy rather than \emph{cap} the directional transfer.
The broader contact-and-friction toolbox these methods draw on is surveyed by
\citet{andrews2022contact}.

\paragraph*{Passivity and energy-stable contact.} Enforced energy properties for
contacting systems are established across several communities, and we claim none
of the mechanisms as new. Haptics: time-domain passivity
control~\citep{hannaford2002} accumulates an energy observer and absorbs net
generated energy, and passive midpoint integration~\citep{kim2017pmi} enforces
discrete-time passivity for real-time LCP contact, including a flexible-beam
example carried on vibration coordinates. Audio: \citet{rath2008} models contact
between vibrating modal objects so that discrete-time contact transitions never
\emph{increase} total energy, in real time at audio rates. Graphics:
\citet{su2009energy} clamp contact/collision energy gain analytically for
frame-rate rigid bodies --- the same quadratic-in-the-scale argument as our
Eq.~\ref{eq:alphaquad}; FEPR~\citep{dinev2018fepr} projects each approximate
step of a PBD/PD solver back to the constant energy--momentum manifold;
\citet{you2026energy} steer an implicit integrator to a prescribed energy
target under barrier contact. Multibody: port-Hamiltonian flexible multibody
dynamics~\citep{brugnoli2021} preserves passivity structurally. None states a
\emph{directional} transfer budget --- each constrains the \emph{system}
(non-increase, whole-system passivity, conservation, an energy target); ours
is one-sided and source-referenced --- modal-storage gain at most $\eta$
times the \emph{measured rigid contact loss} --- enforced cumulatively inside
the shared rigid--modal contact rows.

\paragraph*{Real-time augmented-Lagrangian solvers.}
XPBD~\citep{macklin2016xpbd} casts contact as a compliant constraint --- the
regularized-compliance formulation introduced by \citet{tournier2015stable} ---
and its rigid-body form~\citep{mueller2020xpbd}, together with the VBD/AVBD
descent variants~\citep{chen2024vbd,giles2025avbd}, hosts rigid and soft contact
at a fixed low iteration budget. \citet{macklin2019small} showed that shrinking
the substep, rather than raising the iteration count, is what recovers
stiff-contact accuracy --- the band-limiting trade our $h$-ladder quantifies for
the modal transient (\S\ref{sec:results:fem}). Reduced coordinates have reached
these hosts --- \citet{peng2024reduced} build a reduced XPBD from linear modes
and modal derivatives for soft-robot actuation --- but as a deformation
subspace: no host carries vibration modes as unilateral \emph{contact-row} DOFs
coupled two-way with rigid frames, which is the slot this paper fills.

\paragraph*{Positioning.} Table~\ref{tab:positioning} maps the neighborhood on six
axes. The two-way \emph{mechanism} (columns \textbf{DOF}, \textbf{2-way},
\textbf{Modal}) is shared by several prior methods --- it is \emph{not} our
novelty. Nor is any single column: post-step energy projection already lives in
PBD-class hosts~\citep{dinev2018fepr}, modal subspaces already live in
XPBD~\citep{peng2024reduced}, and energy-stable or passive real-time contact
exists in audio and haptics~\citep{rath2008,kim2017pmi}. The contribution is the
\emph{conjunction} no prior row occupies: vibration modes as contact-row DOFs,
two-way under one multiplier, inside a fixed-budget real-time AL host, with an
enforced \emph{directional} transfer bound --- one-sided and funded by measured
rigid contact loss, rather than whole-system conservation or passivity.
