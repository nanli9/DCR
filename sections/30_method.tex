\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Modes as native contact constraints}
\label{sec:method:constraint}
Each body is a floating frame of reference: state
$(x,R,\amp)$ with COM position $x$, rotation $R$, and modal amplitudes
$\amp\in\mathbb{R}^k$ over mass-normalized free-body modes $\Phimat$ (DCR Eq.~6,
rigid modes discarded). A material point $\bar p$ deforms as
\begin{equation}
  p(x,R,\amp) \;=\; x + R\big(\bar p + \Phimat_{\bar p}\,\amp\big),
  \label{eq:ffr}
\end{equation}
with $\Phimat_{\bar p}$ the $3\times k$ restriction of the basis to that point.
Alongside the Newton--Euler equations, the reduced elastic dynamics are (DCR
Eqs.~5,~7)
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
  M_q\ddot\amp + D_q\dot\amp + K_q\amp = \Phimat^{\!\top} f_{\mathrm{ext}},\\
  M_q=I,\qquad K_q=\operatorname{diag}(\omega_j^2),\qquad
  D_q=\alpha_0 I+\alpha_1 K_q,
\end{gathered}
  \label{eq:reduced}
\end{equation}
diagonal because the modes are mass-normalized --- no reduced inverse is ever
formed, and the whole modal block costs $O(k)$ per update. Differentiating the
normal projection of the two deformed contact points (Eq.~\ref{eq:ffr}) with unit
normal $\nvec$ (from $A$ into $B$, $\lambda_N\ge0$) makes the unilateral gap
\emph{linear in the amplitudes}:
\begin{equation}
  C \;=\; C_{\mathrm{rigid}} \;+\; \big(\Ghat_A\!\cdot\amp_A - \Ghat_B\!\cdot\amp_B\big),
  \qquad \Ghat_X = \nvec^{\!\top} R_X\, \Phimat_X ,
  \label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
where $R_X$ is body $X$'s rotation. Writing $z=(v,\omega)$ for a body's rigid
velocity DOFs, the full gradient of one contact row (lever arms $r_A,r_B$ from each
COM) is the ordinary rigid Jacobian extended by one modal column block per
participant:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial C}{\partial z_A} = \begin{bmatrix}\nvec\\ r_A\!\times\!\nvec\end{bmatrix}\!,
  \;\;
  \frac{\partial C}{\partial z_B} = -\begin{bmatrix}\nvec\\ r_B\!\times\!\nvec\end{bmatrix}\!,
  \;\;
  \frac{\partial C}{\partial \amp_A} = +\Ghat_A,
  \;\;
  \frac{\partial C}{\partial \amp_B} = -\Ghat_B.
  \label{eq:grad}
\end{equation}
A single contact multiplier $f\ge 0$ then (i)
pushes the rigid bodies apart and (ii) drives each participant's modes by
$+\Ghat_A f$ on $A$ and $-\Ghat_B f$ on $B$. Newton's third law is built into
the row --- equal-opposite Jacobian halves under one multiplier, not a
post-solve velocity increment --- so the coupling is two-way and momentum-consistent
\emph{per row}; what a truncated host sweep does to that row on fast impacts
is a solver property independent of the modal coupling, quantified in
\S\ref{sec:results:momentum}. The support contact (body$\leftrightarrow$ground) is the \emph{same} row type
with the ground as degenerate participant: the surface is
$y_{\mathrm{rest}}+U_y^{\!\top}q_s$, so $\partial C/\partial q_s=-U_y$ with $U_y$
the support mode shapes' vertical components at the contact's footprint. That is
the whole generalization --- every body with modes participates, including stacked
bodies that only ever touch another body. Mass-normalized modes give
$M_q=I$, $K_q=\operatorname{diag}(\omega_j^2)$, $D_q=\alpha_0 I+\alpha_1 K_q$
(Rayleigh; DCR Eq.~7), so no reduced inverse is ever formed.

\paragraph*{Relation to the velocity-level law.}
Eq.~\ref{eq:gap} is a transcription, not a new contact law: with $J$ the full
row gradient of Eq.~\ref{eq:grad} (frozen at the linearization point) and
$u=(z_A,z_B,\dot\amp_A,\dot\amp_B)$ the row's stacked generalized velocities,
one linearization step of the gap gives
\begin{equation}
  C^{n+1} \;\approx\; C^{n} + h\,J\,u^{n+1},
  \label{eq:velequiv}
\end{equation}
so the position-level complementarity $0\le C^{n+1}\perp f\ge0$, divided by
$h$, is the velocity-level rigid--modal contact condition of
\citet{zheng2011} up to the stabilization bias $C^{n}/h$ --- the same shared
Jacobian, the same multiplier. If anything the transcription \emph{narrows}
their law: we couple modes through the normal rows only, while
\citet{zheng2011} resolve vibration in the friction stage as well, and a
velocity-level system accommodates Newton restitution directly (DCR adds it
in the right-hand side, their Eq.~4) where the position-level row gives
$e{=}0$ (\S\ref{sec:conclusion}). We therefore claim nothing for the rewrite
itself. What it changes is the \emph{solver} the row must survive: a global
QP converged to tolerance absorbs the stiff modal block silently; the
fixed-budget local sweeps of the real-time hosts do not --- they inject
energy or diverge outright
(\S\ref{sec:method:solve}--\S\ref{sec:method:passivity}), and that failure
and its repair are the content of this paper.

\subsection{Solving the stiff modal block in a local iteration}
\label{sec:method:solve}
A global QP absorbs a stiff modal DOF ($K_q\!\sim\!10^{11}$) implicitly at no
additional cost; a
fixed low-iteration \emph{local} solver makes that stiff, dense modal block diverge
under naive Gauss--Seidel. Making it stable \emph{and} two-way there is the
algorithmic content of this section, and the reason the passivity bound of
\S\ref{sec:method:passivity} is needed. We embed the row of Eq.~\ref{eq:grad} in
both solver families.

\paragraph*{AVBD (augmented Lagrangian).} AVBD steps by minimizing the incremental
potential over substeps; each contact carries a penalty-plus-multiplier force
$f=\max(0,\rho\,C+\lambda)$ whose multiplier $\lambda$ is updated between sweeps,
and each body block solves its local $6\times6$ system with Hessian
$M/h^2+\sum\rho\,J^{\!\top}J$ over its incident rows. Naively adding the $k$ modal
DOFs to the same Gauss--Seidel sweep diverges at production budgets: the modal
block couples \emph{every} contact of a body through the shared $\amp$, and its
stiffness spans $\omega_j^2\sim10^2\!-\!10^{11}$, so sequential scalar updates
amplify error. Instead the amplitudes are solved in a separate
\emph{engaged-gated block} pass after each body sweep. Discretizing
Eq.~\ref{eq:reduced} by backward Euler in the increment
($\dot\amp \to (\amp+\Delta\amp-\amp^{\mathrm{prev}})/h$,
$\ddot\amp \to \Delta\amp/h^2$) and gathering the engaged contact forces
($f>0$; separated rows exert nothing and must not pull) gives the implicit
block solve
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
  \Big(\tfrac{1}{h^2}M_q+\tfrac{1}{h}D_q+K_q\Big)\,\Delta\amp
  = \textstyle\sum_{\mathrm{engaged}}\Ghat^{\!\top} f
        - K_q\,\amp - \tfrac{1}{h}D_q\,(\amp-\amp^{\mathrm{prev}}),\\
  \amp \leftarrow \amp + \omega_r\,\Delta\amp,
\end{gathered}
  \label{eq:qblock}
\end{equation}
with under-relaxation $\omega_r$ (\texttt{modal\_relax}, $0.7$ in production; the
diagonal $M_q,K_q,D_q$ make the solve $k$ independent scalar updates --- the
implicit denominator $1/h^2+(\alpha_0+\alpha_1\omega_j^2)/h+\omega_j^2$ is what
lets $\omega_j^2\sim10^{11}$ coexist with an $8$--$16$-iteration budget). The
under-relaxation is not cosmetic: each pass re-linearizes the contact set against
amplitudes the bodies have not yet reacted to, and $\omega_r$ damps the lag
between the amplitude update and the bodies' reaction. We also evaluated the
textbook alternative --- eliminating
$\Delta\amp$ into the contact row by a cross-term Schur complement, which adds the
modal compliance $\Ghat\,\tilde K^{-1}\Ghat^{\!\top}$ to the row's effective mass.
In a converged solve the two are identical --- we measure the two q-blocks
agreeing to $1\%$ at a $32{\times}4$ budget; but at a truncated iteration count
the Schur row does work against an implied $\Delta\amp$ that is never
back-substituted into the body velocities, and the unmatched half of that work
appears as spurious modal energy. At a $4{\times}1$ budget this is a net-injecting
coupling --- the Schur row's net modal excess reaches $9.4\times10^{3}$~J past
the reservoir while the gated, under-relaxed block-GS stays passive under the
\emph{identical} budget; remove the under-relaxation and both inject, which is
why the explicit, under-relaxed $\Delta\amp$ is what remains passive in practice
(\S\ref{sec:results:passivity}).

\paragraph*{XPBD (compliant position-level).} The same row becomes a compliant
constraint with $\tilde\alpha=\alpha/h^2$ and damping $\gamma$; the generalized
inverse mass simply gains the modal terms of Eq.~\ref{eq:grad},
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
  w \;=\; J M^{-1} J^{\!\top}
        \;+\; \Ghat_A M_{q,A}^{-1}\Ghat_A^{\!\top}
        \;+\; \Ghat_B M_{q,B}^{-1}\Ghat_B^{\!\top},\\
  \Delta\lambda \;=\; \frac{-C-\tilde\alpha\lambda-\gamma\,\dot C}
                            {(1+\gamma)\,w+\tilde\alpha},
\end{gathered}
  \label{eq:xpbd}
\end{equation}
after which positions and amplitudes update along the gradient,
$\Delta z = M^{-1}J^{\!\top}\Delta\lambda$ and
$\Delta\amp_A=+M_{q,A}^{-1}\Ghat_A^{\!\top}\Delta\lambda$ (mass-normalized:
$M_q^{-1}=I$), the modal part scaled by the same under-relaxation. Unlike AVBD,
the modal \emph{internal} dynamics cannot ride an implicit block solve here; each
mode becomes its own compliant row $C_j=\amp_j$ with compliance
$\alpha_j = 1/\omega_j^2$ (so the row's constraint force $-\amp_j/\alpha_j$
reproduces the elastic force $-\omega_j^2\amp_j$) and Rayleigh damping through the
$\gamma$ term of Eq.~\ref{eq:xpbd}. This is exactly XPBD's small-step compliant
form, which is why the energy argument of \S\ref{sec:method:passivity} applies to
it in full. The formulation difference matters operationally: AVBD's implicit
block solve tolerates $\omega_r=0.7$, while XPBD's sequential projections have a
measured ceiling of $\omega_r=0.25$ on the stiff scenes ($0.4$ marginal,
$0.5$--$0.6$ ejects bodies ${\sim}90$~m, ${\ge}0.65$ NaN on the road impact) ---
the same truncation-order sensitivity that motivates the formulation-level
passivity analysis below.

\subsection{The passivity bound}
\label{sec:method:passivity}
Because the local solve under-converges, the contact can inject energy into the
modes. We cap the per-step contact$\to$modal transfer by
\begin{equation}
  \dEmod \;\le\; \eta\,\dErig , \qquad \eta\in[0,1],
  \label{eq:passivity}
\end{equation}
enforced globally across all contacts each rigid step, with
$\dErig$ the rigid kinetic energy lost during contact resolution that step ---
the physical reservoir any vibration must be paid from. Every result in this
paper runs $\eta=1$, the pure passivity point (vibration may consume at most
the energy contact actually dissipated); \S\ref{sec:results:passivity} sweeps
$\eta<1$ and measures what tightening the budget buys. We use
\emph{passivity} in the time-domain sense of \citet{hannaford2002}: the modal
subsystem may gain at most the $\eta$-scaled energy measured leaving the rigid
subsystem through contact resolution. The building blocks --- the exact
quadratic dependence of energy on an update scale~\citep{su2009energy,
you2026energy} and post-step energy projection in a position-based
host~\citep{dinev2018fepr} --- are established mechanisms; the content here is
the port and supply rate (\S\ref{sec:related}).

\paragraph*{The passive scale $\alpha$.}
The step's aggregate contact$\to$modal transfer is a velocity-level increment: the
modes' velocities change by $\dot\amp \leftarrow \dot\amp + \alpha\,s$, where
$s = M_q^{-1}\sum\Ghat^{\!\top}\!j$ collects the projected contact impulses and
$\alpha\in[0,1]$ is the scale to be chosen. The increment leaves $\amp$ (hence the
elastic potential $\tfrac12\amp^{\!\top}K_q\amp$) untouched, so the modal energy
change is purely kinetic and \emph{exactly quadratic} in $\alpha$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
  \dEmod(\alpha)
  = \tfrac12(\dot\amp+\alpha s)^{\!\top} M_q(\dot\amp+\alpha s)
    - \tfrac12\dot\amp^{\!\top} M_q\dot\amp
  = \alpha\, b + \tfrac12\alpha^2 a,\\
  a = s^{\!\top}\! M_q s \ge 0,\qquad
  b = \dot\amp^{\!\top}\! M_q s .
\end{gathered}
  \label{eq:alphaquad}
\end{equation}
No linearization is involved --- Eq.~\ref{eq:alphaquad} is exact, so the bound it
implies is exact. Imposing $\dEmod(\alpha)\le E_{\max}=\eta\,\dErig$ and taking
the largest admissible scale gives the closed form
\begin{equation}
  \alpha^\star \;=\;
  \min\!\Big(1,\; \frac{-b+\sqrt{b^2+2\,a\,E_{\max}}}{a}\Big) \;\in\; [0,1],
  \label{eq:alphastar}
\end{equation}
(the positive root of $\tfrac12 a\alpha^2+b\alpha-E_{\max}=0$; for $a\to0$ the
increment is ineffective and $\alpha^\star=1$). The sign of the cross term $b$ carries
the physics: $b<0$ means the impulse \emph{opposes} the current modal velocity,
so a partial increment first removes energy and a larger $\alpha$ is admissible;
$b>0$ means it adds energy to an already-moving mode, and the square root caps it. Because
$a,b$ are aggregated over all contacts before scaling, a single $\alpha^\star$
bounds the \emph{global} step, closing the
loophole where each contact is individually passive but their sum is not.

\paragraph*{The controller as shipped: cumulative reservoir, exact projection.}
Eq.~\ref{eq:alphastar} caps one velocity-level increment in isolation; the
implementation enforces the \emph{cumulative} form of Eq.~\ref{eq:passivity}.
Each rigid substep deposits the irreversible contact loss into a reservoir,
$r \leftarrow r + \eta\,\max(\dErig,0)$, with $\dErig$ the substep's
gravitational work minus its change in rigid kinetic energy --- zero in
ballistic flight, positive only when contact dissipates --- and the realized
modal gain $g=\max(\Delta E_m,0)$ debits it. While $g$ fits the reservoir the
controller is inert. A substep that would overshoot is projected back by
scaling the full modal state,
\begin{equation}
  (\amp,\dot\amp) \;\leftarrow\; \gamma\,(\amp,\dot\amp), \qquad
  \gamma^2 \;=\; \frac{E_m^{\mathrm{pre}} + r}{E_m^{\mathrm{post}}},
  \label{eq:gammaproj}
\end{equation}
with $E_m=\tfrac12\dot\amp^{\!\top}\!M_q\dot\amp+\tfrac12\amp^{\!\top}\!K_q\amp$
the modal mechanical energy before / after the un-clamped substep. Because
$E_m$ is a homogeneous quadratic in the state,
$E_m(\gamma\amp,\gamma\dot\amp)=\gamma^2E_m(\amp,\dot\amp)$ \emph{exactly}, so
the projected substep realizes precisely its remaining budget --- the clamp
lands on the ceiling, not near it (measured to $\sim\!10^{-17}$~J,
\S\ref{sec:results:passivity}) --- and by induction over substeps every prefix
of every run satisfies
$\sum\dEmod \le \eta\sum\dErig + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{tol}}$. The cumulative
inequality therefore holds \emph{by construction} wherever the controller
runs. Eq.~\ref{eq:alphastar} is its special case when the substep's whole
modal update is the increment $\alpha s$: only $\dot\amp$ is touched and the
projection reduces to the $\alpha$-cap. Scaling $\amp$ together with
$\dot\amp$ is what makes the projection exact for an arbitrary co-solved
update; it is also why an injecting host is over-damped rather than merely
capped (\S\ref{sec:results:passivity}) --- once injected energy mixes into
elastic PE, the ledger cannot separate it from the valid ring.

The architecture is thus deliberately two-level: contact is resolved at the
position / incremental-potential level (\S\ref{sec:method:solve}), while the
controller measures and projects the \emph{realized} modal state at the
velocity--energy level. Bounding realized energy, rather than reformulating
the constraint, is what lets the guarantee hold however far the host
converged --- and why the same controller serves both hosts unchanged.
In practice the two formulations sit on opposite sides of this bound: the AVBD
block solve of Eq.~\ref{eq:qblock} is measured passive at the production
budget (the clamp is a monitor, $0$ activations at $\omega_r=0.7$), while the
XPBD projections of Eq.~\ref{eq:xpbd} inject without it and the clamp is
load-bearing (\S\ref{sec:results:passivity}). This is a deliberate departure
from DCR's forced IIR (their Eq.~10): the modal excitation is funded by
measured rigid kinetic-energy loss, not a forced response.
