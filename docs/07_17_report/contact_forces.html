Two-Way Modal Contact as a Native Constraint — and the Contact Network It Buys

Follow-up on the two-way coupling raised at the last meeting — it is implemented, natively. Every cube now carries its own modal analysis (per-cube FEM eigenmodes, not just the slab), and the modal response is a native constraint: one unilateral contact row on the deformed surface of both bodies, co-solved for rigid poses and modal amplitudes together. A stacked cube feels the ring of the cube beneath it. Two formulas, then the evidence.

0 · Where we left off — the one-way kick baseline

The contact solver ran on rigid bodies only; afterwards the impulses forced the modal oscillators. The ring is a readout — the modes appear in no constraint, so nothing they do can ever change a contact force. The box sits on rigid concrete while a movie of a vibrating surface plays underneath it.

1 · The move — write the contact gap on the deformed surface two-way by construction

A contact constraint is a gap that must stay non-negative. The whole change is one substitution — measure the gap at the surface point including the modal flex:

pX = xX + RX ( rX + ΦX(rX)·aX ) # contact point = rigid pose + mode shapes × amplitudes

Projected on the contact normal, the row gains one linear term per participant:

0 ≤ C = Crigid + ĜA·aA − ĜB·aB ⊥ λ ≥ 0 # ĜX = n̂ᵀ RX ΦX — one number per mode: # how far unit amplitude of mode k moves this # contact point along the contact normal

One shared multiplier λ now serves rigid poses and modal amplitudes — the amplitudes are extra columns of the contact Jacobian, solver unknowns now, not a post-solve read:

Jrow = [ JArigid | ĜA | −JBrigid | −ĜB ] on state ( poseA, aA, poseB, aB ) # one λ, every DOF

Two-way. ∂C/∂a = Ĝ ≠ 0: a flexing mode changes the gap — hence the force — inside the same solve. The ring modulates the support force; a payload mass-loads and detunes the ring.
Momentum-consistent. One λ on one row: what drives A's modes is exactly the reaction B feels. Newton's third law is in the algebra — no post-solve correction, no energy from nowhere.

2 · Symmetry ⇒ a contact network one row type · two special cases

The row is symmetric in A and B — nothing says B must be the ground. A rigid body is the zero-modal-column case; the earlier support contact is the one-sided case. Cube↔slab and cube↔cube are the same row type, so every body can carry a basis — here each cube gets its own modal analysis: its own FEM tet mesh, generalized eigensolve of (K, M), mass-normalized elastic eigenmodes with modal damping. Vibration then travels hop by hop through contacts: slab → base → mid → upper, each hop a physical multiplier. The passivity ledger ΔEmodal ≤ η·ΔErigid loss is enforced globally across the network, not per contact.

Would ABD help? already wired in · interchangeable

(a) normalized top-cube ring: the modal cube and the affine (ABD) cube both transmit a ring through the network; the pure-rigid cube (0 modes) cannot ring. (b) the settled ledger is basis-independent — rigid, modal, and affine all hit the same m·g multiples at every joint.

ABD is a co-rotated affine 9-DOF body whose corner Jacobian Bc substitutes for the modal Φc — it drops into the same rows with no special-casing. But the affine subspace is uniform stretch/shear only: it cannot represent the bending/plate modes that carry the far-field ring, and its flex showed a slow secular buildup over the 3 s run not yet energy-governed.

Position: modal Φ stays the headline basis; ABD is a supported, swappable option and a future-work direction.

The method in three lines

1) p = x + R ( r + Φ·a ) # deformed contact point 2) 0 ≤ Crigid + ĜA·aA − ĜB·aB ⊥ λ ≥ 0 # one shared unilateral row 3) rigid: ±Jᵀλ modes: +ĜAᵀλ , −ĜBᵀλ # same λ routed to every DOF

A rigid body is the zero-column case; the support contact is the one-sided case — cube↔slab and cube↔cube are the same row. That is why it is a network.

On top of this: the §15 passivity ledger is enforced globally across the network, and the same co-solve is validated against an all-FEM shared-operator ground truth on five scenes. Runtime: the RTX 4090 card above.
