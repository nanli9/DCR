# Stage X5 — Performance at PAPER_CONFIG (CPU host)

**Status:** complete for the CPU host path. **Code:**
`benchmarks/paper_eval/x5_perf/run_perf.py`. **Out:** `x5_perf/out/perf.csv` + manifest.

## Scope / honesty

These are **CPU-host** timings at PAPER_CONFIG (relax 0.7, 16×4, symplectic). The
symplectic modal step is host-only (device+symplectic is unsupported upstream), so
the paper config's numbers are CPU. The device-residency / warp-CPU-beats-CUDA
crossover is a *different* axis (non-symplectic path) documented in
`docs/avbd_native/native_dual_solver.md` (warp-CPU 0.9–2.4 ms/step, real-time) — it
is NOT the paper config and is not re-measured here.

Hardware: the session machine (CPU). 100 frames after a 5-frame warm-up.

## Read

- **The X1 passivity clamp is cheap: +1–2 ms/step** (2–5% of step time) across every
  scene-solver — the energy guarantee is nearly free.
- **AVBD is 1.3–2.8× faster than XPBD** on this host path (XPBD's per-mode
  Gauss–Seidel support sweep is the cost; dinner/xpbd at 17 bodies / 24 modes is the
  worst at 162 ms).
- **None of the CPU-host symplectic configs are real-time at 120 Hz** (fastest is
  ledge/avbd at 34.8 steps/s ≈ 29 ms). This is the honest limitation of the paper
  config: correctness (symplectic, host) over speed. The real-time story lives on
  the non-symplectic device/warp-CPU path (separate axis, above).

## Caveats

- The `modal_overhead_ms` column in the CSV compares full-run vs a *frozen-ring*
  baseline (support still solved, just no inertia) — NOT a clean modes-off baseline
  — so it is not a reliable "cost of modes" isolation and reads negative in several
  cells (the frozen BE path takes a different, sometimes slower route). The reliable
  numbers are `mean_ms`, `worst_ms`, and `clamp_overhead_ms`.
