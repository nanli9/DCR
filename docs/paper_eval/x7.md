## Goal

Answer the original paper's admitted flaw (§5.4): *"If the coefficient of
restitution approaches one we will have perfectly elastic collision response and
should not be distributing energy to vibrations."* The paper's forced-IIR modal
response (Eq. 10) injects vibration energy driven by the contact IMPULSE,
independent of restitution — but as ε_r→1 the contact dissipates almost nothing,
so injected-vibration / contact-loss blows past 1 (energy created). The native
constraint funds its modal gain FROM the rigid loss (X1 clamp, foundation §15),
so its ratio is ≤ 1 at every ε_r.

## Two arms (drop scene: pot on a modal table with resting plates)

- **paper-DCR** — `DCRWorld` + `ModalDCRCoupler` (the post-solve forced-IIR
  response) + `ConstraintSolver` (honors restitution, Eq. 4). Sweep the pot's
  ε_r; measure per-impact Σ injected-DCR-KE ÷ Σ rigid-contact-loss (windowed on
  the primary impact — a cumulative-over-run ratio is masked by ERP/settling
  dissipation).
- **native** — the X3 FEM-modal support + the X1 passivity clamp ON. Native XPBD
  contact is hardcoded e=0 (a documented gap — adding a Newton restitution term
  is a real solver change touching the numpy reference at `solver_xpbd.py:1725`
  plus two warp kernel mirrors, out of scope here). But the clamp GUARANTEES
  `modal_gain ≤ rigid_loss` for ANY restitution, so the native ratio is ≤ 1 by
  construction — the bounded reference.

## Honesty notes

- The DCR ratio's jump between ε_r=0.7 and 0.9 is sharp: it reflects the
  per-impact WINDOW (5 rigid steps around peak DCR injection) — once the bounce
  is elastic enough that the pot rebounds cleanly within the window, the measured
  loss drops abruptly. The trend (loss→0, injection persists, ratio→>1) is the
  real, honest signal; the exact crossing ε_r is scene/window-dependent.
- `last_dcr_ke_injected` is the KE the DCR kick adds to the distant (plate)
  bodies — the observable "energy distributed to vibrations" for this
  post-solve-kick arm. Measured against the contact loss it is the energy-balance
  the paper's §5.4 is about.
- The native arm is e=0 (contact), so its curve is a horizontal reference: the
  clamp bounds the ratio ≤ η=1 for ANY restitution because the modal gain is
  debited from the rigid loss — the restitution value cannot change that.
