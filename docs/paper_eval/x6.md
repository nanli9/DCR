## Done — long-horizon passivity (the washing-machine / periodic-forcing demo)

The breadth phenomenon one-way DCR cannot make provable: a bounded cumulative
energy ledger under **sustained forcing over a long horizon**. On the injecting
synthetic-basis shelf at a low budget (4×1 — X1's robustness matrix shows XPBD
injects thousands× here), a sustained impact rings the modal support for 8 s:

| arm | peak ring energy | passive() | Σ modal gain vs Σ rigid loss |
|---|--:|:--:|---|
| **clamp OFF** | **173,830 J** (blows up) | — | unbounded |
| **clamp ON** | **32.85 J** (bounded) | **True** | 32.85 J = 32.85 J (rides the budget) |

- **Un-clamped**, the forced response accumulates and blows up ~4 orders of
  magnitude over 8 s (the paper's non-conservation, sustained).
- **Clamped**, the ring is held at the rigid-loss budget (32.85 J) and stays
  flat + `passive()` for the ENTIRE run — a **5,292× lower** peak. This is the
  long-horizon version of X1: the per-step §15 guarantee holds cumulatively over
  thousands of steps under sustained forcing.

Honesty notes:
- This uses the **synthetic-basis** shelf because that is where the clamp bites.
  The X3 **FEM-modal** support is well-behaved and does **not** inject even at
  4×1 (measured: cum modal gain 0.10 J ≪ rigid loss 2.44 J, clamp inert) — the
  clamp is a safety net for the aggressive bases, exactly as X1 characterized.
- **True periodic re-impact** (teleporting the impactor back up each cycle) is
  NOT wired: moving the DCR body does not move the solver's internal `_X`
  (the documented body-index/state issue), so the impactor would not actually
  re-drop. The sustained single-impact ring is the cleaner passivity probe and is
  what is shown; a genuine periodic-forcing driver needs a solver-level body
  reset (concrete next step).
