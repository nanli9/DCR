# Stage X4 — Solver divergence: chatter ↔ ring (partial)

**Status:** chatter-correlation done; the definitive impulse-train replay is the
remaining piece. **Code:** `benchmarks/paper_eval/x4_divergence/run_chatter.py`.
**Out:** `x4_divergence/out/chatter.csv`.

## What was tested

Hypothesis (from memory `xpbd-vs-avbd-modal-ring-mechanism`): XPBD rings more than
AVBD because its impactor CHATTERS (many rebounds), re-exciting the mode each
landing. This script counts impactor rebounds (force-spike bursts) and correlates
them with the slab-ring energy, at PAPER_CONFIG, on all 3 scenes × 2 solvers.

## Result — the simple chatter hypothesis is REFUTED at PAPER_CONFIG

| scene | solver | impactor rebounds | slab ring [mJ] | two-way | objKE [mJ] |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| shelf | xpbd | 8 | 12,640 | 14.6× | 440 |
| shelf | avbd | 10 | 17,765 | 6.2× | 226 |
| ledge | xpbd | 3 | 84,670 | 97.6× | 93 |
| ledge | avbd | 6 | 67,040 | **0.59×** | **1.9** |
| dinner | xpbd | 35 | 8,568 | 40.9× | 55 |
| dinner | avbd | 28 | 16,359 | 46.6× | 55 |

- **Rebound counts are similar between solvers** (mean XPBD 15.3, AVBD 14.7) — NOT
  the "XPBD chatters much more" picture.
- **corr(rebounds, ring energy) = −0.66** — negative, the opposite sign the
  hypothesis predicts. AVBD often has MORE rebounds and a comparable/larger ring.

So at PAPER_CONFIG (relax 0.7, symplectic — the config the earlier chatter
observation predates), impactor chatter does not explain the XPBD/AVBD ring
difference. (The earlier note likely reflects a different config and/or rebound
metric; verified here, it does not reproduce — logged honestly rather than forced.)

## The real divergence is in TRANSFER, not ring generation

The sharpest case is **ledge/AVBD**: it builds a LARGE ring (67,040 mJ, comparable
to XPBD's 84,670 mJ) yet the resting object gains almost nothing (objKE 1.9 mJ,
two-way 0.59×). The ring is present but does not launch the object. So the
documented "dead coupling" is **not** "no ring" — it is "ring present, not
transferred". This is the genuine open question for the generality claim: why does
an equally-energetic AVBD ledge ring fail to do work on the resting object?

## What remains (the definitive experiment)

The **impulse-train equivalence** test (plan X4 step 2) is the one that cleanly
attributes the divergence: record each solver's per-substep support-row impulse
train, replay BOTH through the identical modal ODE offline, and check whether the
modal response is determined by the impulse train (⇒ constraint is solver-agnostic,
divergence is 100% host contact behaviour) or not. This needs per-substep impulse
logging (a solver hook) — not yet wired. The ledge/AVBD transfer autopsy (mode-shape
magnitude Φ(x_c) at the object's feet; normal-projected support velocity) is the
companion probe.

**Honest takeaway:** the easy chatter explanation fails the test; the divergence is
a transfer/geometry effect that the impulse-replay experiment must resolve.
